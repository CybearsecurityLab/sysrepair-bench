CLAIM 3 -- Cost and efficiency
==============================

PAPER LOCATION
  Section "Results / Cost and efficiency"; Table "tab:cost"; Figure
  "fig:cost-acc".

THE CLAIM UNDER TEST
  On MiniMax-M2.7 at Day-1 K=1, the solvers differ substantially in tokens per
  success and dollars per success, and the accuracy-versus-cost frontier in
  fig:cost-acc is not dominated by the most accurate solver. Dollars use the
  pricing snapshot recorded in the Experimental Setup section: $0.60/M input,
  $2.40/M output, $0.06/M cached input, with cached tokens estimated as
  total - input - output.

WHAT run.sh DOES
  Recomputes tokens/success and dollars/success per solver from the token
  accounting in the shipped logs, regenerates the frontier figure, and diffs the
  table against expected/cost.tsv.

  The pricing constants live in scripts/analysis/pricing.py as a dated
  snapshot, NOT fetched at runtime. Provider prices change, and a claim that
  silently re-prices itself against a live tariff would stop reproducing the
  paper the moment the vendor updated a rate card.

REQUIREMENTS
  NO-CREDENTIALS. NO-GPU. NO-DOCKER.

RUNTIME
  About 5 minutes.

WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE
  run.sh exits 0, the table matches, and the regenerated figure is written to
  observed_cost_acc.pdf for visual comparison against the paper's figure.

HONEST SCOPE
  Cached-token counts are ESTIMATED as total - input - output because the
  provider does not report them separately. That estimate is stated in the
  paper caption and is reproduced here rather than corrected, so the artifact
  matches the publication. Any evaluator recomputing dollars from a different
  cache assumption will get a different absolute cost; the RATIOS between
  solvers, which is what the claim rests on, are insensitive to it.
