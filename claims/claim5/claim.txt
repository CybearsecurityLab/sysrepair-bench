CLAIM 5 -- Failure-mode analysis
================================

PAPER LOCATION
  Section "Results / Failure-mode analysis".

THE CLAIM UNDER TEST
  The failures are dominated by a small number of recurring modes rather than
  being diffuse, and the dominant mode differs between the Day-1 and Zero-Day
  conditions: under Day-1 the failures are predominantly execution failures,
  and under Zero-Day they are predominantly localisation failures.

WHAT run.sh DOES
  Recomputes the failure-mode distribution from the shipped logs by classifying
  every failing episode from its oracle verdict components and its transcript,
  then diffs the distribution against expected/failure_modes.tsv.

  Classification is rule-based and the rules are in
  artifact/analysis/failure_modes.py, deliberately not an LLM judge. An
  LLM-judged taxonomy would not reproduce deterministically, and a
  failure-mode table that changes between runs cannot be an evaluated claim.
  The rules key off the oracle's structured output: which checks failed, whether
  the security check or the availability check or both, and whether the
  transcript contains a submit attempt at all.

REQUIREMENTS
  NO-CREDENTIALS. NO-GPU. NO-DOCKER.

RUNTIME
  About 10 minutes.

WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE
  run.sh exits 0 and the distribution matches within tolerance.

HONEST SCOPE
  The taxonomy is coarse by construction. "Localisation failure" means the agent
  never produced a change that touched the vulnerable surface, inferred from the
  oracle's per-check output; it is not a claim about the agent's internal
  reasoning. The paper's qualitative reading of these modes rests on transcript
  excerpts that are shipped in artifact/transcripts/ for inspection, but the
  quantitative distribution reproduced here is what the claim is scored on.
